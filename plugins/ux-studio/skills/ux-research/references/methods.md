# Methods: when to use each, and how many people

Sample sizes are conventions from common practice, not guarantees. State them as such and name the risk of going below them. No numbers below come from this product's data.

| Method | Use it to | Do not use it to | Sample (convention) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stakeholder interview | Learn goals, constraints and vocabulary of the business | Learn what end users do | 3-6 stakeholders |
| User interview | Understand tasks, context, pain points, language | Measure how many people do something | 5-8 per role |
| Contextual observation | See the real workflow, workarounds, devices | Test a design | 3-5 sessions per role |
| Survey | Size how common a behaviour or opinion is, after interviews gave the options | Discover problems you have not named yet | Enough responses for the cuts you need; as a convention 30+ per segment for rough comparisons |
| Card sort | Learn how users group and name content | Validate a finished navigation | 15-20 participants (open sort), 5-8 minimum for a first read |
| Tree test | Check findability in a proposed sitemap | Judge visual design | 30+ participants for stable percentages; 8-10 for a quick check |
| Usability test (moderated) | Find the problems that stop a task in a prototype or app | Prove preference or market fit | 5 per role and iteration; repeat after fixes |
| Unmoderated usability test | Compare task success and time across many users | Understand why something failed | 20+ |
| Analytics / log review | See what people really do and how often | Explain why | All available data; state the period |
| Diary study | Capture needs that happen over days | Quick answers | 5-10 for 1-2 weeks |

## Choosing

1. Start from the decision the research must support; write it as a question.
2. Unknown problem space: qualitative first (interviews, observation). Known options: quantitative (survey, tree test).
3. Existing product: check analytics and logs before asking people.
4. Prototype ready: usability test with the real tasks and success criteria.
5. Budget and access decide the rest; say what is lost when a method is dropped.

## Source

Sample sizes above are convention. Cite a source only when it is confirmed: none is asserted here.

## Usability metrics

Task success (completed / attempted), time on task, errors and recoveries, and a post-task rating if the team already uses one. Define the success criterion per task before the session.
